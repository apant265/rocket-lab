# Flight timestamp analysis

Engineering has been removed from the header. Open **Simulate**, choose a time on the bottom timeline, and use **CFD / Thermal / Stress** in the right inspector. **Back / Forward** use the adjacent step size, from 0.001 to 60 seconds. Left/right arrows also step when a text field does not have focus. Scrubbing or editing **T+ seconds** pauses playback. Home rewinds; Apogee seeks to the recorded event. Camera and trajectory controls remain independent of seeking.

**Built-in** defaults to hardware GPU Euler flow and material heat diffusion. The inspector names the renderer adapter; completed flow results name the independently selected compute adapter. **Use GPU (required)** stops on a GPU error or thermal accuracy failure; uncheck it to deliberately use the CPU. **External (CPU)** selects actual OpenFOAM flow/thermal and CalculiX fin FEA. Flight dynamics and beam stress integration use CPU f64. See [external solver setup and physical scope](external-solvers.md).

Drag either sidebar's inner edge to change its width, and drag the bottom timeline/analysis panel's top edge to change its height. Sizes persist while the app is open. The bottom panel keeps its chosen height as charts update. Sidebars reserve a separate scrollbar gutter, with text clipped before the handle. Long property labels wrap while their units remain visible. Launch-condition units use prominent 20-point labels in both Imperial and Metric, including wind, weather and cloud fields. Parts uses one scroll area; the palette reflows as its width changes. Charts grow with the panel, and scrollable content stays accessible at smaller sizes. Panel limits preserve space for the central view. The compact header starts with Build and Simulate; Dark and Light use a shared neutral palette with sage accents.

Model export, launch integration and timestamp analysis display elapsed time and approximate remaining time. Flight runs on a background worker so these controls stay responsive. CFD counts completed iteration batches; thermal counts completed heating-history frames. GPU batches are awaited before reporting progress. Their ETA extrapolates the current phase's measured rate, so initialization, CPU verification and saving can add time. Blender and external solvers use the median of up to seven successful previous matching runs; a first run says **Estimating remaining time**. Historical overruns explicitly update the estimate rather than showing a false completion. Timing history is local in `output/runtime-estimates.json`; estimates are approximate and depend on geometry, flight duration, resolution and hardware.

Choose a computational detail level and press **Compute at … s**. The job captures the flight configuration, exact timestamp, analysis mode, detail and GPU preference. Playback and camera motion stay responsive. A changed timestamp or settings displays a stale-result notice. A job completed for a superseded design is discarded. Results and a manifest containing the actual grid, backend, integration step and runtime are saved under `output/snapshots/`.

| Detail | CFD cells / iterations | Thermal axial + fin cells per stage | Maximum diffusion step | Maximum heating-boundary interval | Fin beam divisions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preview | 96 × 64 / 1,000 | 8 + 2 | 0.05 s | 0.2 s | 32 |
| Standard | 384 × 256 / 6,000 | 64 + 16 | 0.00625 s | 0.05 s | 256 |
| Fine | 1024 × 512 / 16,000 | 128 + 32 | 0.003125 s | 0.025 s | 512 |
| Ultra | 2048 × 1024 / 32,000 | 256 + 64 | 0.001 s | 0.0125 s | 1024 |

Thermal and fin stress resolution are doubled at Standard, Fine and Ultra. The thermal GPU supports up to 1024 material columns; extra component splits or additional stages can exceed this limit and are reported as errors. The material solver still has two through-wall finite volumes; the wall-layer control applies to external diffusion meshes.

Ultra computes 2,097,152 flow cells, 10.7 times the previous Ultra grid, with 3.2 times the iteration budget. The viewer and external CLI share the same presets. External vehicle shells increase to 96 circumferential elements at Ultra. Full-resolution CSV/PNG outputs preserve the field; the SVG overview is sampled to a bounded preview size.

The built-in thermal model keeps two finite-volume cells through each wall. External OpenFOAM uses 2/8/16/32 wall cells and displays outer/inner half-wall averages. Extra components add columns. The explicit axial-conduction stability limit can require smaller steps than the preset maximum. GPU thermal supports at most 1024 columns and device-dependent storage limits. Exceeding those limits produces an explicit error; reduce detail or deliberately disable GPU to run the requested grid on the CPU. Large grids and long heating histories can take substantial time.

CFD uses continuous telemetry interpolated at the requested timestamp, holding the previous recorded state across stage discontinuities. It solves the remaining attached body section at Mach ≥ 0.05. The inline pressure image and velocity arrows are computed fields. The viewport's pressure and airflow modes remain separately labeled geometric estimates.

Thermal starts from the launch initial temperature and integrates the entire recorded history **up to** the requested time. It preserves recorded knots and event times and subdivides boundary intervals at higher detail. Future heating is excluded. Rewinding within that computed prefix can reuse the thermal history; moving beyond it hides the temperature overlay until a new calculation covers the time. The Analysis result includes outer/inner wall strips, per-region material temperatures and CSV export.

Stress first computes the thermal prefix, then covers every retained body/nose section and planar fin region with its assigned material. Built-in uses CPU annular beam/shell screening; External uses bonded CalculiX S8 shell sections and separate primary C3D20R fin solids. Vehicle shape is the default stress image; 3D stress covers the same regions. Enter measured structural properties per material for stiffness/strength results. Missing data stays absent. [Loads, materials, scope and verification](vehicle-stress-and-units.md).

This refines post-processing of the existing recorded flight; it does not re-integrate flight dynamics. Built-in CFD remains a planar inviscid perfect-gas Euler model. External flow adds OpenFOAM laminar viscosity and a no-slip adiabatic wall, with the same planar section limitation. Thermal uses prescribed heating rather than CFD-resolved wall flux. External stress is static shell/primary-fin FEA and built-in stress is beam/shell screening. Neither resolves full vehicle joints, laminate failure, shell buckling or flutter. Grid refinement reduces discretization error within a model; it does not establish physical fidelity. See [NASA's grid-convergence guidance](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html) and [model/error distinctions](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/errors.html).

Verification includes exact rectangular-cantilever stress and deflection convergence, stage-discontinuity handling, a causal thermal prefix, and heat-capacity/contact conservation under thermal mesh refinement. The native smoke run exercises seeking, timestamp invalidation, two CFD grids, thermal propagation, and refined stress rendering.


## Large 2D result images

In **Simulate → Analysis**, **2D images** opens the central field viewer; **3D vehicle** restores the rocket view. Compute CFD, Thermal or Stress in the right inspector at the selected timestamp. The 2D viewer shows the captured timestamp/backend/detail, and marks changes in time or settings. Flow and stress retain their captured result until recomputed; thermal can rewind within the computed prefix.

- CFD: pressure coefficient, absolute pressure in kPa, solved air speed in m/s, or Mach. Flow motion uses RK2 tracers through bilinearly sampled computed velocities, with trails and relative local speed retained. Playback slows one freestream transit to six display seconds; this animates a captured field, not transient turbulence or liquid physics. Optional arrows use the computed velocity field. The CFD silhouette follows the continuous nose/body/transition profile, with a vector outline at every zoom and subpixel coverage in PNG exports. Texture-only wall-cell gaps are reconstructed from the nearest solved fluid row; hover and CSV keep the actual solved-cell mask. The numerical wall is still Cartesian/stair-stepped, so this fixes displayed edges without claiming a body-fitted solver. Flow textures use linear sampling for smooth display; thermal and stress vehicle views render colored triangles at every zoom. PNG exports retain native field pixels and exclude the motion overlay. The map preserves the physical axial/crossflow aspect ratio and masks solid cells.
- Thermal: **Vehicle shape** is the default. It projects the actual exported mesh, including nose, body and fins, and maps the computed material-region temperatures onto it. Choose the retained vehicle or an individual stage, and toggle outer/inner temperature. Detached stages disappear from the retained vehicle image; uncomputed parts are gray and the background is transparent in PNG exports. Hover reports the mapped region, material, conductivity and thickness. A matching exported mesh is required. **Wall section** keeps separate outer/inner half-wall averages; **Heating history** shows axial station versus time through the viewed timestamp. The geometry view interpolates recorded temperatures in time and interpolates neighboring computed columns within each component and material, preserving property interfaces. It does not add thermal degrees of freedom or resolve circumferential gradients. The existing wall/history views sample solved columns and frames.
- Stress: Vehicle shape shows the full retained structural envelope with section materials. Fin envelope shows the primary trapezoidal fin's span-wise stress envelope in MPa, masked to its outline. CalculiX values are maxima over each imported span bin; preview values come from the beam calculation. Color is constant across each chord, rather than a resolved chordwise FEA contour. Full local 3D stress fields are still in the saved solver case.

Wheel zooms the image, drag pans, **Fit image** resets framing, and hover reports physical coordinates and field values. Each image has a color scale with actual units. Cp uses −0.3 to 1.5; other maps use their displayed range, so compare numbers as well as colors across runs.

**3D vehicle** uses the same captured CFD scalars, thermal interpolation, stress envelope, scope, layer choice and color ranges as the 2D vehicle view. CFD displays the computed pressure/velocity slice behind the 3D vehicle and projects its planar field through the z direction onto the exported surfaces; it does not introduce circumferential or resolved fin-flow data. Before a flow calculation, the separately labeled incidence estimate remains available. Flow and stress show their captured time until recomputed; thermal clamps viewing to its computed prefix. Both geometry views subdivide the exported triangles for smooth field transitions without changing geometry or adding solver degrees of freedom. Vehicle PNG exports use a 4096-pixel-wide raster.

**3D vehicle** also maps computed temperatures onto the exported mesh, with an automatic temperature range shown in the inspector. This makes small temperature changes visible. Its inner-material toggle changes the sampled layer. Gray marks parts without a computed thermal region. Both geometry views visualize the existing material model; they do not constitute a full 3D conjugate heat-transfer solution.

**Save PNG + metadata** writes the native raster and a matching JSON sidecar under `output/analysis-images/`. The sidecar includes units, axes, scale, captured and viewed timestamps, backend and map scope. Exporting at a rewound thermal time names and labels the actual viewed time. PNG pixel count follows the represented data/raster and does not imply a finer solver mesh.

To open directly and compute a GPU flow image at four seconds using the Preview mesh:

```powershell
rocket-lab.exe view --mission examples/kate-anvil-desert.json --analysis-view --start-time 4
```


The Kate and Anvil case at four seconds passed the external Preview-mesh image check. A Standard-mesh run at that timestamp produced an OpenFOAM floating-point exception; its logs are preserved in `output/snapshots/r0-t4010000000000000-cfd-standard-OpenFoam/`. The 2D viewer reports external failures and shows no substitute field. Higher detail alone does not guarantee solver stability or a validated prediction; this case needs further numerical investigation.
