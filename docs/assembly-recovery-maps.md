# Parts, recovery and geographic maps

The latest extension adds independent exterior tube/nose/transition/fin components, undo/redo, explicit OpenRocket stage mapping, geographic elevation/weather profiles, reefing and an optional moving canopy with spring/damper tether. [Advanced laboratory](advanced-laboratory.md) specifies input semantics and limitations; the baseline behavior below remains available.

Start `./Launch.ps1` for the **Kate and Anvil / 5 lb desert study**. This preserves the older CDX1 total assembled mass and first moment by allocating 5 lb out of the top stage's dry assembly to the named payload. The payload station is an assumption. The example uses illustrative New Mexico coordinates (32.99°, −106.975°) and 1,376 m site height; this is not an established launch location. The original source mapping remains available separately.

## Assembly

The left palette contains Blender-rendered model images in Structure, Systems and Internals catalogues. Drag a card into the 3D viewport to see a translucent component preview snapped to the assembly axis. The cursor's camera ray determines the stage and axial station, so placement follows camera movement and zoom. Internal stations snap to 1 cm and clamp the entire component inside the chosen stage. Release to attach. Alternatively click a card, then click in 3D to place it; Esc cancels. Click a placed component to open its inspector. **Move in 3D** arms an existing extra component for another placement, including moving it between stages. **Internals** exposes internal components for selection.

The draft assembly updates immediately when parts are placed, moved or resized. Tubes have open bores, rings have holes, nose profiles follow the selected shape, fins use the configured trapezoid and electronics have a board proxy. Internals makes the shell translucent; placement reveals it temporarily. Catalogue images depict generic models, while the 3D draft and placement meshes use configured dimensions. The evaluated Blender exterior and flight are rebuilt automatically by Launch simulation. A theme-colored selection box outlines the selected visible component.

Body tubes and transitions snap above or below existing sections and extend the selected stage. A nose attaches at the top. Dropping a **transition** adds an editable taper in the current stage, without creating a booster. Inner tubes attach inside the body; centering rings surround their parent inner tube and can automatically fit the body bore. Motor selection is available on inner-tube motor mounts, filtered by bore and length. Click a component in 3D to edit dimensions, offsets, material and mass. Kate + Anvil assigns the specified 5 lb payload. See [file exchange and builder attachments](model-exchange.md) for the current workflow.

The palette includes inner/motor tubes, couplers, bulkheads, centering rings, rail buttons, shock cords, ballast and avionics bays. Each added part stores material density, dimensions, axial location and independent mass/CG overrides. Tubes and rings use annular cylindrical volumes; bulkheads use solid disks. Packed cords/electronics and ballast default to measured-mass mode. Add/remove and move parts, then **Launch simulation**. **Internals** makes the exterior translucent and reveals their schematic positions. Internal geometry is rendered by Bevy alongside the headless Blender exterior.

Rocket mass fields and telemetry use **lb**; material density is displayed in **lb/ft³**. Computation and mission JSON retain SI, with 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. Length remains in metres. Vehicle overview lists **motors by stage**, and the payload card shows its combined weight. Motor thrust curves still retain their physical impulse internally.

Material presets are editable engineering defaults for fiberglass/G10, carbon composite, aluminum, plywood, balsa, cardboard, PLA, steel and nylon. Density editing creates a custom material. **Calculate dry mass from materials** replaces measured stage dry mass with estimated airframe/nose/fins plus entered other hardware. Airframes use hollow cylinders beginning behind a front transition or nose, fins use trapezoidal prisms, and curved nose shells currently use a thin conical approximation. A front transition contributes its own shell mass rather than overlapping a full cylindrical shell. Other dry hardware must include empty motor casing and any hardware not represented by added components. Avoid counting the same item twice. Added palette parts contribute separately in both mass modes. Mass, CG and cylinder-distribution inertia feed the actual solver. Surface finish is persisted metadata and does not alter imported drag data.

This implements a useful subset of OpenRocket component attributes, including density, shell thickness, mass/CG overrides, materials, payloads, motor curves and recovery. Full OpenRocket feature parity is not claimed: arbitrary fin profiles, per-part drag overrides, complete nested import fidelity and aerodynamic interactions remain outside this version. Transition, tube, nose and trapezoidal fin components are supported; structural and thermal screening limits are described in the advanced laboratory guide. The source behavior is described in the [OpenRocket component documentation](https://wiki.openrocket.info/Common_Component_Characteristics) and [override documentation](https://openrocket.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/overrides_and_surface_finish.html).

## Per-stage parachutes

Enable **Internals** and click the packed parachute within the appropriate stage, or click its visible **Recovery preview** canopy. The upper retained vehicle uses the mission recovery settings. Each booster has its own optional `recovery` configuration applied after separation. The viewer supplies explicit starter booster settings when loading a legacy design without them; the original CLI configurations with no stage recovery remain ballistic. Starter booster main areas are estimates for approximately 6 m/s descent before propellant burn. They are not imported parachute specifications.

Each detached stage detects its own descent using an ideal truth-state recovery controller, subject to its battery lifetime, configured deployment altitude, Mach/q limits, canopy inflation and line-load limits. The retained vehicle still uses its sampled/filtered/latency-delayed avionics controller. Main deployment replaces drogue drag. Released motors are quenched. The solver keeps propagating unfinished detached vehicles after retained touchdown until all bodies land or the mission duration ends. `summary.completed` continues to mean retained-vehicle touchdown; inspect detached CSV/events for complete booster recovery.

Canopies use an open scalloped fabric dome with alternating gore panels, a central vent, seams and sixteen suspension lines. Inflation animates deployed size and the canopy aligns with descent. The **Recovery preview** toggle in Build shows each configured stage canopy. In Simulate, the follow selector tracks the retained vehicle or either detached stage. Canopies disappear after detached touchdown. Geometry is a visualization: no fabric, explicit tether pendulum, opening shock, reefing, wake shielding or canopy heating dynamics are solved. Chute drag remains `q Cd A × inflation fraction`, with an effective nose suspension attachment producing a stabilizing body torque.

`detached-stages.csv` includes per-stage drogue/main fractions, q, Mach, line load, velocity and touchdown speed. Events identify the stage responsible for deployment, deferral, failure and landing.

## Satellite imagery

Enter launch latitude, longitude and site height in **Simulate → Launch conditions** and apply the mission. Below the weather controls, choose a satellite provider, map span and date, then **Load satellite**. Fetching happens in a worker thread and the UI remains responsive. Imagery is available as a textured ground layer in 3D and as an original image with geographic tracks and landing markers under **Map & landings**. **Top-down flight view** provides a plan view of the flight. A 160 km region covers the default desert study's approximately 59 km downrange path. The map window also lists landing coordinates and a Google Maps link.

- **NASA MODIS:** public true-color satellite imagery from [NASA EOSDIS GIBS WMS](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/python-usage/), dated 2025-06-15 by default, approximately 250 m source resolution. Clouds and incomplete daily coverage may be visible. NASA PNG/bounds/provenance are cached in `output/site` for offline reuse. A matching NASA cache loads automatically on startup.
- **Google satellite:** the official [Google Maps Static API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/maps-static/start), requiring the user's `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY`, enabled API and billing project. Set the environment variable before launching. Only explicit Load clicks request imagery; the key is never saved in mission files or included in displayed request errors. Images stay in memory, retain the original logos/attribution and use their Web Mercator bounds. The live Google provider requires a configured key for verification.
- **Offline PNG/JPEG:** supply a local image and west/south/east/north bounds in degrees. Select Web Mercator only for imagery in that projection. Image dimensions are limited to 4,096 pixels and encoded inputs to 16 MiB. An unreferenced screenshot cannot identify its own geographic location.

For a headless NASA download:

```powershell
cargo run --features viewer -- satellite --latitude 32.99 --longitude -106.975 --span-km 160 --date 2025-06-15
```

Coordinates use the same 6,371 km spherical Earth and launch ENU frame as the integrator, preserving longitude, latitude and MSL height. NASA uses geographic image bounds; Google uses Mercator bounds. Regional imagery is supported within ±80° latitude without crossing the date line. This imports imagery, **not elevation terrain**. Ground contact stays on the constant-site-height sphere; geographic landing points therefore do not resolve local hills or mountains. Landings are model predictions for the selected weather and coefficient assumptions.

Flight export adds **trajectory.geojson**, with retained and detached geographic tracks and landing points for every landed body. It can be imported into a GIS tool alongside map imagery. The original CFD/hypersonic limitations continue to apply; see [model.md](model.md).
