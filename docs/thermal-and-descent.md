# Material heat diffusion and descent attitude

The optional moving-canopy extension now integrates point-canopy position/velocity and spring/damper tether forces with the rigid body, including suspension torque. The baseline suspension approximation described below remains the default when no canopy mass is allocated; fabric/fluid dynamics remain absent. See [advanced-laboratory.md](advanced-laboratory.md) for the coupled option and its assumptions.

In **Build**, choose a stage's airframe, nose or fin material and expand **Heat transfer properties**. Conductivity, specific heat, emissivity and a temperature screening limit are editable. Material density and wall thickness determine heat capacity; these thermal properties apply even when measured dry mass is selected. Internal component mass overrides set their heat capacity. Effective isotropic composite properties are starter estimates, not laminate or resin specifications.

In **Simulate**, choose a flight timestamp, select **Thermal** in the right analysis controls, choose a detail level and click **Compute**. The mesh shows a fixed 0–600 °C temperature scale. **Inner wall map** switches to the second cell through the wall. The inline Analysis result contains an axial outer/inner strip, region peak temperatures, material limits and CSV export. Red table values exceed the entered screening limit; exceeding a limit does not automatically break or ablate the vehicle. The **Apogee** button pauses at the integrated apogee event.

For a visible descent-rotation example start `rocket-lab.exe view --mission examples/ballistic-tumble.json`, launch, press **Apogee**, then **Play**. This synthetic HPR example has both recovery canopies deliberately disabled, wind and the specified 5 lb payload. **Attitude & rotation** shows the integrated pitch, yaw and roll rates and axis tilt. Regular recovery canopies can stabilize the body; disabling them in this example isolates the ballistic descent behavior.

The thermal worker uses the actual Bevy GPU device through wgpu 27 and a WGSL compute shader. This machine's adapter is an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Ti. It checks the first 64 frames against an independent f64 CPU implementation and reports an error if the difference exceeds 0.25 K or the requested GPU grid is unsupported. A cooperative workgroup handles up to 1024 columns with synchronized double-buffered lane batches. Long histories exceeding GPU storage limits report an error; explicitly disable GPU for a CPU solve. The GPU toggle applies to the next Compute request. The main 6DoF flight integrator remains deterministic CPU f64.

Standard detail uses sixteen axial shell columns and four root-to-tip fin columns per stage, plus added internal components. Preview/Fine/Ultra adjust these grids and integration steps; see [timestamp detail presets](timestamp-analysis.md). Each column contains two finite-volume wall cells. Capacity is ρ cp A t / 2 and radial conductance is 2 k A / t. Backward Euler solves the radial 2×2 system exactly at each substep; axial and contact exchange are conservative explicit terms, with a stability-limited step at most 0.05 s. The radiation conductance is linearized as εσA(T + Tair)(T² + Tair²). Adjoining shells have an estimated coupler connection, cut when the actual flight separates them. Fin roots connect to the shell. Component contacts assume 1% contact area and a 2 mm effective path; these are explicit engineering assumptions, not imported joint measurements.

Flight Mach, wind-relative speed, angle of attack and atmospheric density/temperature supply boundary conditions. Detached bodies use their own motion and attitude. Nose profile slope and shape affect incidence, surface area and shell capacity. Recovery temperature is Tair(1 + 0.9 × 0.2 M²). Convective coefficients combine a laminar flat-plate estimate with a shape/incidence-weighted Sutton–Graves stagnation estimate. The latter is based on [NASA TR R-376](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720003329); applying it over the vehicle with an incidence factor is this project's approximation. Radiation removes heat and the recovery-temperature formulation reduces incoming convection as the skin warms.

These are axisymmetric thermal averages. Optional `experiments.thermal` laws now supply separate effective through-wall/axial conductivities and prescribed regional motor heat; this is not a full laminate conduction tensor or motor combustion solver. Timestamp analysis adds a separate planar inviscid Euler flow tool and tapered-fin stress/deflection screening. The CLI thermal command also provides post-flight energy-bound ablation screening. There is no resolved wall shock-layer heating, Navier–Stokes/turbulence solver, circumferential conduction, pyrolysis, mass-loss feedback or structural fracture. Parachute fabric heating and electronics power dissipation are absent. The atmosphere extension above 86 km and continuum heating in rarefied flow retain their limitations. Thermal diffusion and structural screening do not alter flight forces or avionics. The legacy lumped flight temperature remains separate and is still used by its original screening warning. [Advanced options and exact limits](advanced-laboratory.md).

The worker exports `output/thermal/thermal.csv`, `thermal-model.json`, `mission.json` and `compute.json`. A full independent CPU run is available:

```powershell
cargo run -- thermal --mission output/thermal/mission.json --out output/thermal-cpu
```

Descent attitude follows the integrated quaternion and body angular velocity. Small-angle ascent uses the supplied normal-force slope and CP. Beyond 15° incidence it blends toward crossflow drag on the side projection of the body and configured fins, with a projected-area centroid. Tail-first transverse drag no longer changes sign, so a perturbed fin-stabilized vehicle can become unstable on descent. A nose suspension attachment adds a restoring moment when a canopy inflates. Pitch/roll damping and gyroscopic coupling continue to act through the actual inertia. There is no spin or tumble animation injected at apogee. A nearly symmetric body with negligible angular momentum in rarefied flow can retain its orientation until aerodynamic or recovery forces act. Large-angle crossflow and suspension are approximations and require validation against flight data; fabric and pendulum dynamics remain unsolved.
