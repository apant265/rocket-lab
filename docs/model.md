# Equations and conventions

This page describes the baseline equations. Optional `environment` and `experiments` extensions add geographic terrain, imported wind/air profiles, Mach/alpha/Re coefficient grids, a rarefied drag bridge, moving-canopy tether dynamics, reefing, timed faults and command rules. [Advanced laboratory](advanced-laboratory.md) defines their semantics and limitations. The spatial thermal and planar CFD solvers are separate from the baseline scalar flight-temperature state.

The world frame is a fixed launch-tangent East/North/Up basis that rotates with Earth when Earth-rotation effects are enabled. The spherical Earth center is `(0, 0, -(R + site_altitude))`; altitude comes from radial distance rather than world Z. ENU positions are the CG position of the retained body. Ground contact uses the imported geographic elevation grid when present, otherwise the sphere at launch-site radius. The constant rotation vector is `[0, Ω cos(latitude), Ω sin(latitude)]`. Gravity is central gravitational attraction and rotation adds Coriolis and centrifugal acceleration. Earth's oblateness is not included.

Body Z points toward the nose. Quaternion `q` rotates body vectors into the world frame. CG and CP stations increase **aft from the nose**. Consequently the CP lever arm is `body_Z * (CG_station - CP_station)` and a CP aft of the CG produces a restoring moment at small angle of attack.

The state is `[position_enu(3), velocity_enu(3), quaternion_xyzw(4), omega_body(3), skin_temperature(1)]`. The derivative is evaluated four times per RK4 step. Quaternion normalization occurs at derivative evaluations and the final step. Forces depend on current state, propellant remaining at the RK4 stage time, local density, air-relative velocity and active-stack coefficient data.

```
position_dot = velocity
velocity_dot = force_world / mass + gravity - 2 Ω×velocity - Ω×(Ω×r)
omega_dot = (torque_body - omega×(I omega)) / I
q_dot = 0.5 q * [omega_body, 0]
```

The inertia is diagonal in an axisymmetric approximation. Each mass allocation is represented as a cylinder; transverse inertia includes its displacement from the active CG. Fuel remaining is proportional to the thrust curve's unconsumed impulse. This assumes constant effective exhaust velocity over the measured curve. Motors with variable mass flow need a separate mass-history model. The angular momentum carried out by burned propellant is assumed to cancel the explicit `I_dot * omega` term; motor jet damping is omitted. This assumption is not a general open-system dynamics solution.

Air-relative velocity is velocity minus wind. Drag acts opposite that velocity. The lateral normal force uses `CN_alpha * sin(alpha) * cos(alpha)` in the body lateral plane; it matches the input small-angle slope but is only a bounded engineering extension at large alpha. Axial drag also receives an `0.8 sin²(alpha)` extension. Pitch/yaw/roll damping scales as `q A L² / (2 V)`. Fin cant adds an approximate roll-forcing term, not a detailed fin/body model. The scalar coefficient table does not capture independent yaw/pitch asymmetry, shock interactions, Reynolds dependence or rarefied-flow corrections.

On the rail, motion is projected onto the launch axis and attitude is held fixed. A pad stop prevents negative rail motion. Rail release occurs at the integration boundary following the rail-length crossing. Mach, altitude, apogee and ground events likewise have timing error bounded by the effective step. Motor-curve corners and commanded time/burnout boundaries split the RK4 step. Angular damping and canopy drag shorten the requested timestep when necessary. This is a deterministic bounded-step integrator, not a general local-error-controlled RK45 solver.

At separation, active-stack CG positions are changed to the new body CGs. Local rotational-point velocity is preserved, and the separation velocity increment is distributed according to body mass to conserve linear momentum. The detached body's unburned fuel stays with it, but its motor is quenched. Detached-body aerodynamic CP is approximated at 70% of its own length; its other aero data remain inherited engineering approximations. Rigid-body angular rates are preserved; no collision, off-axis separation impulse or spin transfer is modeled. CG sphere-ground intersection ends a detached trajectory. Optional per-stage recovery now applies after separation, using an independent ideal truth-state descent controller with battery, Mach/q, inflation and line-load checks. See assembly-recovery-maps.md.

The computer samples altitude at a configured rate and delivers filtered readings through a latency queue. Barometer altitude is used below its ceiling; otherwise an idealized GNSS altitude sample is used within its ceiling. Altitude noise is deterministic sinusoidal disturbance, useful for repeatability but not a stochastic hardware noise spectrum. Descending readings must occur for a configured number of consecutive samples before recovery commands are allowed. No valid altitude or no remaining battery disables those commands. Upper-stage ignition also checks measured altitude and truth-derived tilt. IMU range flags represent sensor saturation; there is no estimator consuming IMU measurements or actual firmware execution.

Baseline chute area ramps linearly during inflation; drag is `q * Cd * area * inflation_fraction`. Commands wait for the q/Mach envelope; line-load exceedance fails the canopy and a main replaces the drogue. Optional recovery models add two-ramp reefing, an empirical opening factor and a moving canopy mass with unilateral spring/damper tether and attachment torque, integrated with the body by RK4. Bag extraction, wake shielding, canopy porosity, fabric/fluid motion and heating are omitted.

Atmosphere lapse layers follow the 1976 standard-atmosphere form through 84.852 km geopotential altitude. Above that, a continuous 7 km scale-height pressure extension is used, with a domain warning above 86 km geometric altitude. The equation of state is perfect-gas air with constant gamma. Mean free path and body-diameter Knudsen number identify continuum breakdown; the solver flags `Kn > 0.01` but does not correct aerodynamic coefficients.

Stagnation heating uses `1.83e-4 * sqrt(rho / nose_radius_m) * speed_mps³`, in W/m², as an Earth-air Sutton–Graves screening approximation. A single thermal node receives this flux over `heated_area_m2` and loses gray-body radiation to atmospheric temperature. Heating is not attitude-dependent. It does not resolve catalytic walls, high-enthalpy chemistry, ablation, individual fin leading edges or thermal stress. Configured q/g/temperature limits report exceedances while the rigid body continues to fly.

Reference comparison converts exported altitude and speed from feet to SI, then linearly interpolates simulated altitude at overlapping reference times. Peak differences and altitude RMSE are descriptive statistics, not an accuracy certificate. The input export's altitude is assumed to be AGL; confirm the RASAero output convention for a different source. Flight trace CD/CP values are trajectory samples, not a universal independent coefficient database.

Dispersion uses seeded SplitMix64 plus Box–Muller normal sampling. Drag, thrust, dry mass, two horizontal wind components and launch elevation are independent demonstration distributions. Correlated manufacturing tolerances, random gust histories, ignition reliability and probabilistic break-up are not represented.
